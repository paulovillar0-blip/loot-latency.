60Hz vs 120Hz for Gaming

Understand refresh rate before upgrading

A 120Hz display can feel smoother than a 60Hz display, but it is not an automatic performance upgrade. The experience depends on the game's frame rate, the display's response behavior, and the device's ability to sustain performance.

What the numbers actually mean

Refresh rate is how many times a display can update each second. At 60Hz, a refresh interval is about 16.7 milliseconds; at 120Hz, it is about 8.3 milliseconds. More frequent updates can reduce the time between opportunities to show a new frame. These intervals do not by themselves measure total input latency.

Refresh rate is not frame rate

Refresh rate describes the screen, while FPS describes how many frames the game renders. A game capped at 60 FPS does not magically produce 120 unique frames on a 120Hz panel. Some games or devices restrict supported frame rates, so check the actual settings rather than assuming the display's specification tells the whole story.

Why frame pacing matters

A stable 60 FPS can feel better than a higher average FPS with frequent spikes. Uneven frame delivery causes visible judder or stutter. If you notice irregular freezes, our stutter versus lag guide can help separate rendering problems from connection issues.

Response time and motion clarity

Two displays with the same refresh rate can look different because pixel response times, blur, overshoot, and processing vary. A low-quality 120Hz panel is not necessarily clearer than every good 60Hz panel. Read detailed independent measurements when comparing monitors, not only the advertised Hz number.

Does 120Hz help competitive gaming?

Higher refresh rates can improve motion tracking and reduce some display-related delay when the game outputs enough frames. Competitive shooters and fast action games may benefit more than slower or turn-based games. Skill, connection quality, controller settings, and stable performance still matter.

Battery and heat on phones

Running a high-refresh display and rendering more frames can use additional power, though the effect depends on the screen technology and software. Adaptive refresh may reduce power use when the content is static. If your phone warms up during long sessions, try a lower frame-rate target and compare sustained performance; see our phone gaming tips.

Before you upgrade

Check whether your favorite games support higher frame rates, whether your device can maintain them, and whether the display has good response characteristics. Test in person if possible. For everyday browsing and fast games, the difference may be noticeable; for a tight budget, other upgrades may deliver more value.

Final takeaway

120Hz offers more frequent refresh opportunities, but smooth gaming requires a capable device and consistent frames. Choose based on the whole experience rather than a single specification.
